    EXPLANATION

    What is HEI-2020?

    Healthy Eating Index is a measure of diet quality. It describes how closely a set of foods aligns with US dietary guidance.

    The score combines 13 components, including vegetables, fruits, whole grains, protein foods, dairy foods and fatty acids. It also assesses moderation in sodium, added sugars, saturated fat and refined grains.

    Each component contributes to a maximum of 100 points. Many components are standardized for energy intake; an HEI score is therefore not a percentage of daily vitamin requirements.

    The score describes the foods you entered. Recording only part of a day does not describe habitual dietary intake. HEI characterizes the entered foods; it is not a clinical assessment of health.

    Official HEI-2020 scoring criteria · US National Cancer Institute ↗

    EXPLANATION

    What does the diet-structure model show?

    Diet structure refers to the proportions of major food groups and selected characteristics of the foods entered. This model helps illustrate which groups are represented, their balance and what contributes to the overall score.

    It draws on the principles of the Harvard Healthy Eating Plate: approximately half a plate of vegetables and fruit, one quarter whole grains and one quarter healthy protein sources. This is a visual guide to assembling a meal, not a precise prescription in grams, calories or volume.

    VesninMed uses a separate computational model inspired by this plate. It compares food-group quantities by weight and also incorporates selected HEI-2020 components, protein intake relative to a reference, the dairy group and associated nutrients, and other selected food-composition characteristics.

    This produces six components. Each receives a score from 0 to 100 and contributes to the total with the following weights: plant foods 25%; grains 20%; protein 20%; dairy and calcium 15%; components to limit 10%; and food-group proportions 10%. For example, 80 points in a 20%-weighted component contribute 16 points overall. Open the cards below the chart for the exact rules.

    Why is the calculation approximate? Foods of the same weight can differ substantially in energy and nutrient content. The proportions illustrated by the Harvard Healthy Eating Plate were never intended for exact conversion into grams. Our comparison is an exploratory aid, not a validated substitute for the original guidance.

    The structure score incorporates some HEI-2020 components, so the two chart axes are not independent. The VesninMed model's weights have not been independently validated. The 70-point threshold and chart colors are display conventions only. This is an informational estimate, not a clinical assessment of health.

    Harvard Healthy Eating Plate · original source ↗
    CALCULATION METHOD

    How the energy and nutrient chart is constructed

    This chart compares nutrient intake from the foods entered with calculated dietary reference values. It helps explore dietary composition. It does not measure laboratory nutritional status or establish a clinical diagnosis.

    1. Calculate nutrient intake

    For each food, the nutrient content per 100 g is multiplied by the consumed portion weight. Contributions are then added across foods:

    Ii = Σ (mj / 100 × cij)

    Ii — intake of nutrient i; mj — weight of food j in grams; cij — amount of nutrient i per 100 g of food j. Missing composition data must not be treated as a verified zero.

    2. Compare with an applicable reference

    Pi = 100% × Ii / Ri

    Ri — the reference value used for this nutrient in the current VesninMed profile. It may be an RDA, an AI or another calculated reference. A value of 100% means that intake matches this reference, not a universal threshold of health.

    Exceeding 100% does not in itself imply excessive intake. A single day's intake below an RDA or AI does not establish deficiency. RDA and AI have different scientific meanings and must be interpreted accordingly.

    3. Plot the radial chart

    ri = min(158; Pi)

    Each nutrient has its own radial axis. Its plotted distance from the center represents the percentage calculated above. Values above 158% are capped at the outer edge; the actual percentage remains available in the details. Sectors group vitamins, minerals and other substances: group averages never replace individual nutrient values.

    Axis positions and connecting lines make patterns easier to see; polygon area is not calculated as a separate diet-quality index.

    4. Calculate a separate energy circle

    PE = 100% × Ediet / Ereference

    Energy from the entered foods is compared with the requirement calculated for the current profile. The circle uses the same percentage scale as the radial axes. If only part of a day is recorded, a small circle is not evidence of inadequate nutrition.

    Fat and carbohydrate are displayed alongside it. Approximate energy shares are calculated as 100% × (9 × grams of fat / dietary energy) and 100% × (4 × grams of carbohydrate / dietary energy). These are general Atwater factors; individual foods may use different energy calculations, so the resulting percentages need not sum to 100%.

    Acceptable macronutrient distribution ranges (AMDRs) exist for fat and carbohydrate, while an individually prescribed clinical target may differ. Therefore these nutrients are not plotted on additional axes with a misleading “below 100% is bad” interpretation.

    5. Check when color warnings apply

    Green indicates that the selected reference has been reached; amber indicates a value below it. Preliminary reference data are shown with muted color and hatching. These display cues do not classify disease.

    Red is used only when suitable data show an exceedance of a tolerable upper intake level (UL) applicable to total intake of the nutrient. For example, limits for retinol, synthetic folate or supplemental magnesium cannot be applied to total food intake without additional information.

    6. How potential food additions are compared

    To illustrate adding 100 g of a food, the system uses its recorded composition to estimate how intake of the selected nutrient and other nutrients would change. Your diet remains unchanged. Missing values are not assumed to be zero. Examples drawn from unreviewed entries are marked separately.
